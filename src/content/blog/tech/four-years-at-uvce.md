## The Stone Arch of K.R. Circle

Walking through the gates of the University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering (UVCE) for the first time is an experience that stays with you. Located in the bustling heart of Bangalore at K.R. Circle, the college carries over a century of history founded by the legendary Sir M. Visvesvaraya in 1917. The aged stone buildings, high ceilings, and century-old hallways speak of decades of engineers who walked these corridors before you.

When I entered UVCE as an eager Computer Science undergraduate, I had expectations shaped by popular college depictions. What I found was something much raw, much more authentic: an institution where you aren't spoon-fed, where autonomy is thrust upon you, and where you quickly discover that your growth depends entirely on what you choose to build.

## The Early Days: Navigating the Fog

The initial semesters were a humbling reset. Coming out of competitive pre-university entrance exams, I was suddenly confronted with foundational computer science: discrete mathematics, digital logic, data structures, and computer organization.

Like many undergraduates, I experimented with algorithmic programming early on, but without steady direction, I often hit roadblocks. The turning point arrived when I realized that computer science truly comes alive when you stop treating it like an exam syllabus and start treating it as a creative medium.

That shift drove me toward software engineering, full-stack web development, and systems design. I wanted to build things that real human beings could interact with, test, and use.

## Community and the Hackathon Circuit

If UVCE gives you one superpower, it is the culture of peer-to-peer learning and community. Without the rigid corporate packaging of private universities, UVCE students learn to build initiatives from the ground up through clubs, tech societies, and collaborative circles.

One of the defining chapters of my college life was teaming up with my peers to form **WeCode**—alongside Prajwal D P, Kavyashree Devraj, and Samarth Sutrave.

During the **Bit N Build** 24-hour hackathon organized by GDG UVCE × GDG CRCE, we conceptualized and engineered **Digital Closet**, a web application aimed at cataloging personal wardrobes, intelligent outfit suggestions, and community clothing donations. Surviving 24 hours of nonstop caffeinated debugging, architectural decisions, and final pitching, we won the **State Level Web Development Track** and advanced as one of the top 30 teams to the International finals in Mumbai.

That hackathon was more than a trophy; it proved to us that we had the technical chops and teamwork to build under intense pressure.

## Beyond the Classroom

Engineering at UVCE was never confined to 9-to-4 lectures. It happened during:

- **Debates and brainstorming sessions** at the canteen and nearby K.R. Circle spots.
- **Late-night debugging marathons** before lab submissions, troubleshooting compiler errors in C++ and Node.js.
- **Self-directed research and side projects**, exploring deep fake detection techniques and algorithmic foundations.
- **Serving the community**, which eventually led to my tenure as a Student Placement Coordinator, giving back to the very institution that shaped my path.

## Looking Back

Four years sound like an eternity when you're a fresher sitting on the stone benches, wondering where your degree will take you. In reality, it flashes by in a blink.

UVCE did not just hand me an engineering degree in Computer Science; it taught me resilience, resourcefulness, and self-reliance. It gave me lifelong friends, trusted collaborators, and an unshakeable belief that with curiosity and hard work, no technical problem is insurmountable.

To UVCE, the faculty, my batchmates, and everyone who was part of this chapter—thank you for four unforgettable years.
